## Design intent — the section/genre question

Every certified constraint so far treats the manuscript as one corpus: A10's boundary and
within-word residues, A13's multi-distance certification, the generator exclusions — all
pooled statistics. But the manuscript is not presented as one text. Its codicological
sections (herbal, balneological, astronomical, zodiac, pharmaceutical, stars/recipes,
cosmological, text-only) have been treated as genre divisions for a century, and the
campaign had already logged section-shaped anomalies: A7's opening enrichment is
*balneo-negative*, B4's pharma enrichment is section-and-hand coextensive, U4 found balneo
has the tightest line pitch and herbal the loosest. The unasked preregistered question:
**is the certified sequential residue structure itself uniform across sections, or is the
pooled corpus an average over genuinely different regimes?** "Several texts bound
together" vs "one homogeneous system" is a fork every translation theory must take — and
nobody had put it under certification law.
